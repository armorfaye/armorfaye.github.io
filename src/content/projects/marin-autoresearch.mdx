## The problem

There are far more plausible architecture ideas than anyone can test, and most
of them fail. Testing one is a fixed routine: implement it, train a small model
against a control, read the curves, decide what next. That routine is regular
enough for agents to run. The hard part is making a cheap test predict the
expensive one.

[Marin](https://marin.community)’s **Belay** process supplies the expensive
test. Each proposed idea trains for 480 seconds on 8×H100 against the current
recipe and is judged at **equal time**, so a slower idea trains on fewer tokens.
Winners then move up a ladder of larger models.

## The loop

| Stage | Where | Time | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screen | TPU v4-32 | ~12 min | Does it lower loss at all? |
| Speedrun | Belay, 8×H100 | 1–2 h | Does it win at equal time? |
| Ladder | Belay, d512 → d1280 | days | Does the gain hold with scale? |

1. **Propose.** An agent writes each idea as a card: the mechanism, the evidence
   behind it, its cost, and a stop line it must beat partway through training.
   Cards are checked against Belay’s registry, so tested ideas are never repeated.
2. **Implement.** The idea becomes a default-off flag, so the control is the same
   code with the flag off. Tests confirm the flag-off model is unchanged and no
   future tokens leak.
3. **Screen.** A short TPU run against the control. Runs that miss their stop
   line are killed early.
4. **Price.** A TPU gain is discounted by its expected H100 slowdown. Only ideas
   that should still win at equal time are posted.
5. **Climb.** Belay runs the speedrun. Wins go up the ladder, and every result
   comes back to recalibrate the screen.

## Keeping the screen honest

The loop is only as good as the screen’s ability to predict H100. Every Belay
result is compared with its TPU screen, and the pricing is re-fitted from those
pairs. This caught a real problem: an n-gram feature that looked strong on TPU
ran 9% slower on H100 and lost. The agents traced the cost to how the feature
was built, rebuilt it inside the embedding table, and the same idea became the
loop’s best win.
